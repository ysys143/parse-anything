01Verdict

Ranking blends objective text fidelity (against the born-digital text layer) with a page-by-page vision judge. Size did not predict quality — the two 0.9B models place first and second on raw text.

  1. 1GLM-OCR zai-org · 0.9BWinner
    Top on both axes — best char-similarity (0.881) and highest judge average (4.75). Clean LaTeX, correct subscripts, right reading order. Tiny and fastest.
  2. 2DeepSeek-OCR deepseek-ai · 3BAll-rounder
    Flawless equations, lowest hallucination among the leaders. Slightly sparse on the figure page and a few truncations. Grounding tokens preserved.
  3. 3chandra-2 datalab-to · ~4BBest numbers
    Near-perfect number recall (0.998) — strongest at preserving digits. Emits HTML+bbox layout; occasional invented detail on figures.
  4. 4Nanonets-OCR2 nanonets · 3.75BHigh ceiling, unstable
    Ties GLM on the judge (4.75) when it behaves — but degenerates into a repeating "!" loop on some pages (coverage 1.9×, highest hallucination). Great or broken, per page.
  5. 5olmOCR-2-FP8 allenai · 7BUnder-delivers for 7B
    Best on the figure page (flags is_diagram), but misses a third of the document's numbers (recall 0.685) and drops headers/footers. Largest model, middling result.
  6. 6PaddleOCR-VL PaddlePaddle · 0.9BNeeds its pipeline
    parse-anything's native paddle backend. Solid on plain body text, but single-shot mode gives no LaTeX (flat unicode) and loops on the figure page. Its layout pipeline would lift this.
  7. 7Nemotron-Parse-v1.2 nvidia · 0.9BUnusable here
    Scored 0 on all four judged pages — emits bbox tokens then collapses into hallucinated repeating tables. Needs its own postprocessing pipeline, not raw single-shot serving.
  8. —Unlimited-OCR baidu · 3.3BDid not deploy
    Could not be served on the L4 in-budget: no flash-attn wheel for its torch build, and its vLLM path exits at startup even with deps added. The hardest of the eight to run.

04What actually happened on the L4

Deployment was most of the work. Every model needed a different serving fix; the notes below are the ones that mattered.

GLM-OCR · Nanonets · DeepSeek · chandra

vLLM · OpenAI /v1

Served on vllm-openai. Nanonets gave pure "!" garbage on :latest (a Qwen2.5-VL regression) and had to be pinned to v0.11.2. DeepSeek 400'd until max_tokens fit its 8k context.

olmOCR-2-FP8

vLLM · FP8 on Ada

FP8 weights run natively on the L4 (sm89). Works out of the box on v0.11.2 — just slow-ish and weak on numbers.

PaddleOCR-VL

vLLM · trust_remote_code

Arch recognized on :latest. But vLLM's endpoint is element-level; full-page markdown needs the official paddleocr doc_parser layout pipeline. Run here single-shot.

Nemotron-Parse-v1.2

vLLM · custom image

Swapped in for the detection-only nemotron-ocr-v2. Needed a baked chat template + extra deps + max_tokens<9000. Serves, but output is unusable without its postprocessor.

Unlimited-OCR

did not deploy

flash-attn has no wheel for its torch build; the vLLM path recognizes the arch but exits at startup even after adding addict/matplotlib. Left as a documented failure.

PP-OCRv6-det · nemotron-ocr-v2

excluded / swapped

Detection-only models (boxes, not text) — out of scope for a transcription comparison. Nemotron-Parse-v1.2 stood in for the NVIDIA slot.

05How it was measured

  • Integration. parse-anything's --primary paddle transcriber speaks a 3-endpoint PaddleOCR jobs API. A shim mimics it and routes each page to whichever model is up — zero changes to parse-anything.
  • Throughput. Naïve one-page-at-a-time serving is batch=1 and starves the GPU. A pre-transcription pass fires all 46 pages concurrently so vLLM continuous-batches them, then parse-anything replays from cache — ~5× faster (GLM born: 393s → 71s).
  • Documents. A 46-page PLOS Biology paper, born-digital, plus an image-only rasterization (text layer stripped) to force unaided OCR. Same content, so the born text layer is ground truth for both.
  • Caveats. char-similarity is measured against pdftext, which itself garbles math and multi-column order — read the objective scores as relative. PaddleOCR-VL and Nemotron-Parse are structured/pipeline models shown here in raw single-shot mode, which under-represents them.
